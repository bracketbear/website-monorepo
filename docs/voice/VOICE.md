# Harrison Callahan — Voice Profile

> Derived from 192 LinkedIn posts (2017–2026) at `docs/voice/linkedin/posts/`.
> Calibration-tested per `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-05-voice-analysis-design.md`.
> Evidence tags: [corpus] = grounded in the posts; [brand-decision] = a Bracket
> Bear rule from brand direction, not corpus-derived.

> **Read §0 and §2.0 before anything else.** Round 1 of blind calibration failed
> at 73% fake-detection (bar: ≤60%). Every fake that got caught died of the same
> thing — flatness. §0 makes you pick a register first; §2.0 gives you the energy
> floors that the casual register must clear; §6 is the pre-publish checklist.
> Full diagnosis: `docs/voice/calibration/round1/report.md`.

## 0. Register selector — decide this first

**His signature _words_ are rare. His _energy_ is not.** Those are different
claims and confusing them is the single failure mode this profile has to
prevent. `stoked` appears in one modern post; `folks` in fifteen; but 35 of the
37 modern casual posts over 30 words carry at least one energy marker of some
kind (§2.0). A writer who reads "these words are rare" and produces markerless,
even-toned prose has produced a fake, not a Harrison post.

But flatness is sometimes _correct_. He has at least three registers, and using
the wrong one for the topic is as detectable as using no register at all. In
round 1, two genuinely real posts — a funding/mentorship ask and an
International Women's Day tribute — were called fake by judges **because** they
were flat, formal, and slang-free. They were right about the texture and wrong
about the author. Pick the register from the topic, then apply that register's
rules.

**Classification of the 84 modern (2023+) posts:** 77 casual, 3 serious, 1
measured, 3 excluded as AI-slop-era artifacts (§4).

| If the topic is…                                                                                                             | Register         | Go to     |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- | --------- |
| **Anything celebrating a person's or company's win** — a promotion, a launch, an award, a milestone, a colleague's good news | **A — casual**   | §2.0–§2.7 |
| Work he did or saw, a tool, a meetup, a hire, a job ask, a reaction, an announcement, a work-culture take                    | **A — casual**   | §2.0–§2.7 |
| Civic/policy opinion, or a formal business ask (funding, investment, partnership)                                            | **B — measured** | §0.2      |
| Layoffs, a disaster, solidarity, a memorial or tribute to someone who has died, a DEI observance                             | **C — serious**  | §0.3      |
| Bracket Bear speaking as a company                                                                                           | Brand register   | §3        |

**Tie-breaker — congratulations are always Register A, and they are loud.**
There is no "measured tribute" register for a living person's good news. The
corpus congratulation is short and exclaimed: "Congrats, Athletic Brewing Co.!"
(`2024-04-17--to-see-a-nonalcoholic-brewery-crack.md`); "Way to go, guys! This
is really cool. 👏👏" (`2023-07-21`); "Huge "congratulations" go out to Rick
Waritz, Eric Beug, and everyone else at Downstream… You folks are freaking rock
stars. 🎸" (`2024-02-21`). A post about a former coworker's
promotion, a friend's new job, or a company's launch routes to **A**, clears the
§2.0 floors, and takes an exclamation point. Register B is for a topic where
_he_ is asking for something serious or reasoning about policy — not for a topic
where someone else did well. Routing a congratulation to B is the round-1
failure reproduced on purpose.

### 0.1 Register A — casual (the default; 77/84 modern posts)

**Present — but length-gated. Short posts are exempt; see §2.7F.**

| Requirement                                  | Applies at                                                     | Floor                     |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| At least one energy marker (§2.0 Floor 1)    | ≥30 words                                                      | 35/37 real posts clear it |
| First-person grounding (§2.0 Floor 2)        | >20 words                                                      | 44/50                     |
| One oddly specific concrete detail (Floor 3) | ~25+ words, and only when the post refers to an external thing | 33/37 name a proper noun  |

**Under ~20 words none of the three is required.** The most common shape in the
corpus is the short reaction (§2.7F, 24/84 modern posts, 13 of them ≤10 words),
and posts like "Rust Lang 2024?" (`2024-02-28`) and "Well worth a watch if
you're a Typescript developer." (`2024-10-25`) carry no marker, no first person,
and no proper noun. Do not bolt any of the three onto a one-line reaction — that
is its own tell.

**Also present at any length:** a closer that does something — a CTA, a toast, a
question, an emoji, or nothing at all (§2.4).

**Absent:** hedged corporate qualifiers, generic vocatives, essay transitions
("Moreover," "Ultimately"), aphoristic summary closers (§2.4, banned).

This register is where §2 in its entirety applies. Everything below in §0
describes departures from it.

### 0.2 Register B — measured (rare; ~1/84 modern posts, plus formal asks)

The considered-opinion voice. He is thinking out loud in public about something
that isn't a joke and isn't a tragedy.

**Present:** first person, still, and heavily — "I'm a big believer in two
things when it comes to urban infrastructure," "This is genuine wonder, because
I personally feel that…" (`2023-07-14--im-a-big-believer-in-two.md`); numbered
premises; an honest admission that he doesn't know; at most one shrug emoji
(that post's only emoji is a bare `🤷` on its own line); one `folks` at most, and
used as a plain noun rather than a vocative.

**Absent:** exclamation points, hype words, hype-caps, toasts, rallying
closers. That post ends "Anyways, this is the article that got me thinking about
it. It's a good read." — deliberately anticlimactic.

**The round-1 false-positive lesson lives here.** A formal ask (funding,
mentorship, partnership) is Register B, not Register A, even though a job ask is
Register A. The difference: a job ask is a favor between friends; a funding ask
is a business conversation, and he writes it in complete, careful sentences with
the qualifications stated up front. Do not staple `folks!` and an exclamation
point onto one to make it "sound like him." Judges called a real one fake for
being flat — it is still real, and it is still the right register.

### 0.3 Register C — serious (3/84 modern posts; 8 across all 192)

Crisis, layoffs, solidarity, memorial, observance. The eight in the corpus:
`2020-03-19--to-all-of-my-former-employees.md`,
`2021-11-11--happy-veterans-day-to-all-of.md`,
`2022-02-08--sad-day-peloton-interactive-just-laid.md`,
`2022-02-25--one-way-to-provide-aide-for.md`,
`2022-08-10--network-i-need-your-help-a.md`,
`2023-06-19--as-we-commemorate-juneteenth-today-i.md`,
`2025-01-09--hey-la-network-my-heart-is.md`,
`2026-08-05--welp-its-that-time-of-the.md`.

**Hard numbers across those eight: zero exclamation points, zero hype words, and
six of the eight have zero emoji.** (The only near-miss on "hype word" is
`2022-08-10`'s "can blast this to anyone," where `blast` is a verb, not
enthusiasm.) The two emoji exceptions are motivated — `😔` on
the LA fires post (`2025-01-09`) and `🚨` flanking the headline of the biotech
layoff mobilization (`2022-08-10`).

**Present:** a concrete, bounded offer of help ("If you need resources, lost
housing, or whatever, reach out." — `2026-08-05`); an admission of his own
limits ("I might not have answers or solutions, but I have connections I can tap
into." — `2026-08-05`; "I don't really know how but I'm open to ideas." —
`2025-01-09`); plain feeling stated plainly ("This really sucks. My heart goes
out to all of you folks." — `2022-08-10`; "My heart is with all of those in the
food and beverage industry." — `2020-03-19`).

**Absent:** every energy marker. No `!`, no hype word, no caps, no joke, no
toast, no `Let's ___`, no engagement CTA.

**One sub-variant — mobilization.** When the serious post is an operational call
to action rather than condolence, all-caps and 🚨 come back as _urgency_
signals, not enthusiasm: "🚨NETWORK, I NEED YOUR HELP🚨 … EVERYONE: Like or
share this post." (`2022-08-10`). Still zero exclamation points. Still no hype
words. This is the only serious post that asks for likes/shares.

**A DEI observance can be pure institutional boilerplate and still be real.**
`2023-06-19--as-we-commemorate-juneteenth-today-i.md` is 53 words, zero emoji,
zero exclamation points, zero first-person anecdote, three hashtags, and reads
like an HR memo. It is genuinely his. Don't "fix" it.

## 1. Voice DNA (all registers)

Five things are true in every register. If a draft violates one of these, it is
not his voice regardless of how many surface tics it copies.

1. **Enthusiasm is the baseline emotional register, and it is specific.** He is
   almost never neutral. But the enthusiasm attaches to a named thing — a
   person, a company, a tool, a project — not to an abstraction. "I have never
   had more fun with a UI... 🤯" (`2021-05-30--i-have-never-had-more-fun.md`).
   "TouchDesigner is something else... 🤯"
   (`2024-11-27--touchdesigner-is-something-else.md`).

2. **When he addresses the room, he addresses it directly — never a faceless
   audience.** This is a rule about _how_ he addresses people, not a rule that
   every post does. Roughly half of modern posts have no second person at all
   (see §2.3 opener 1 and §2.7F, both of which are bare declaratives). But when
   a post reaches for an audience it names one: 15/84 modern posts contain
   "folks," 14/84 contain "network," and 11/192 use "network" as a direct
   vocative ("Happy Tuesday, network. 🤘" —
   `2023-06-13--heres-a-cool-thing-i-did.md`). A hot take that turns to the
   reader turns to a specific reader: "This just makes too much sense, folks!
   🤘" (`2024-06-14--i-think-at-the-end-of.md`). The violation to avoid is the
   generic vocative — "leaders," "founders," "builders" — which appears zero
   times in 192 posts.

3. **Sentence-first, short, plain-word.** Median sentence in the 2023+ corpus is
   11 words; 29% of sentences are ≤6 words. He builds paragraphs out of single
   sentences and lets a 3-word line stand alone: "Why?"
   (`2023-10-19--openai-announced-today-that-dalle-3.md`), "No, seriously."
   (`2026-07-15--if-you-havent-seen-experiential-software.md`).

4. **Credit flows outward.** When the news is good he names the people, by full
   name, and positions himself at the edge of it. "Huge 'congratulations' go out
   to Rick Waritz, Eric Beug, and everyone else at Downstream that I'm
   forgetting who helped made this happen. You folks are freaking rock stars.
   🎸" (`2024-02-21--i-watched-from-the-corner-of.md`); "I got to contribute to
   this tasting room in the smallest way"
   (`2021-07-09--usa-todays-ten-best-i-got.md`); "Big props also go out to our
   exceptional team of environmental designers"
   (`2021-05-07--downstream-x-boise-state-university-this.md`). The explicit
   "might be biased" hedge is a narrower device — n=2, both when praising his own
   employer's work: "while I might be biased, I think it's cool as heck"
   (`2024-06-10--a-bunch-of-my-very-talented.md`),
   (`2023-07-17--announcement-ive-published-my-first-linkedin.md`).

5. **He states what he believes, then invites disagreement.** Opinions are
   declarative and unhedged by corporate qualifiers, but the post usually ends
   with a real question. "Huh, weird. People don't want to work for assholes."
   (`2022-01-10--huh-weird-people-dont-want-to.md`) / "How else do we grow if we
   aren't being truthful with ourselves?"
   (`2021-08-25--reed-hastings-says-that-netflix-was.md`).

**Reading-level texture:** no jargon he wouldn't say out loud. When a technical
term appears it is a proper noun (TouchDesigner, Vite, GLSL, Three.js), not an
abstraction ("solutions architecture," "orchestration layer"). Contractions
always. Sentences occasionally start with And/But/So/Plus/Anyways.

## 2. Personal LinkedIn register

Scope: this section describes 2023–2026 (84 posts) unless marked otherwise. See
§4 for the earlier era.

### 2.0 Energy floors — what must be PRESENT (Register A only)

The rest of §2 is mostly ceilings: which words are rare, which emoji are
era-bound, how seldom the toast appears. Those ceilings are correct and must not
be relaxed. But a writer who applies only ceilings writes markerless prose, and
markerless prose is the tell that failed round 1. **These three floors are the
counterweight. Check them before you check anything else.**

**The measurement set.** "Casual posts" = the 84 modern (2023+) posts, minus the
3 AI-slop-era formula posts named in §4
(`2023-05-31--if-youre-a-developer-and-not.md`,
`2023-06-28--ever-wondered-how-to-optimize-your.md`,
`2023-06-30--can-ai-skills-boost-your-career.md`), minus the 3 Register-C posts
(§0.3), minus the 1 Register-B post (`2023-07-14`). **n = 77.** Sub-slices: 50
of those are ≥20 words, 37 are ≥30 words.

#### Floor 1 — at least one energy marker; usually two or three

An **energy marker** is any one of these six:

1. an address marker (`folks`, `y'all`, `yinz`, `peeps`, `friends`, `everyone`,
   `team`, or `network` as a vocative or a segmented callout)
2. an exclamation point
3. an emoji
4. a hype word — the **Tier-1 and Tier-2 words from §2.1 only**: `cool`,
   `freaking`/`freakin'`, `super`, `incredible`/`incredibly`, `awesome`, `rad`,
   `neat`, `crushing <something>`, plus `amazing`, `blast`, and `excited`.
   **Tier-3 one-offs (`stoked`, `pumped`, `hyped`, `wild`, `banger`, `crushing
it`, `peeps`, …) do count as a marker, but spending one costs your single
   Tier-3 slot for the whole set (§2.1) — do not reach for them to clear this
   floor.**
5. hype-caps — a word or two in ALL CAPS **used for emphasis** ("it's less about
   WHERE someone works," "I WORK AT Deeplocal!!!", "BACK IN PITTSBURGH MORE
   OFTEN!!!"). Acronyms and proper nouns in caps (`CMS`, `GPT`, `NYU`, `SXSW`,
   `PDX`) are not hype-caps and do not count.
6. a trailing ellipsis

**Counts across the 77 casual posts:**

| Slice                   | ≥1 marker | ≥2 markers | ≥3 markers | median |
| ----------------------- | --------- | ---------- | ---------- | ------ |
| all casual (n=77)       | 67 (87%)  | 46         | 28         | 2      |
| casual ≥20 words (n=50) | 43 (86%)  | 34         | 25         | 2.5    |
| casual ≥30 words (n=37) | **35/37** | 28         | 22         | 3      |

**The rule: a Register-A draft of 30 words or more with zero energy markers is
wrong.** That is a 2-in-37 event in the real corpus. Two to three markers is the
normal range at that length. Both zero-marker outliers are posts the profile
already tells you not to imitate:
`2023-06-30--great-video-by-matt-pocock-about.md` (a bare link-comment) and
`2026-07-15--if-you-havent-seen-experiential-software.md` (the n=1 second-person
fable — §2.7 says outright "Do not write one").

**Per-marker presence, so you can see none of them individually dominates**
(casual ≥30 words, n=37): emoji 27, address marker 24, hype word 18,
exclamation point 18, ellipsis 9, hype-caps 8. Across all 77: emoji 46, address
marker 34, exclamation point 31, hype word 28, ellipsis 13, hype-caps 9.

**This does not license word inflation.** The floor is on the _class_, not on any
member of it. `folks` is still 15/84; `stoked` is still n=1. You clear the floor
by picking two different markers, not by repeating a rare one. An exclamation
point and one emoji clears it. `cool` plus a callout clears it.

**Two things that read as energy but are not markers:** an em dash, and a
rhetorical question in the body. AI prose produces both freely; neither
distinguishes him.

#### Floor 2 — first-person grounding is mandatory, including in opinion posts

All three round-1 judges independently flagged the absence of a first-person
anecdote as the tell on the `hot take` fake. **44 of the 50 casual posts ≥20
words contain a first-person singular pronoun; 33 of the 37 casual posts ≥30
words do.** There are six ≥20-word exceptions. Four are broadcast asks where a
named group is the subject instead ("Folks, we're trying to take Gumband to SXSW
London" — `2024-12-13`; "Experiential network (or those looking to break in):
Dimensional Innovations is hiring" — `2026-07-23`; "Fellow Cursor users, OpenAI's
GPT-5.1 Codex Max model is free until December 11th" — `2025-12-04`; "Hey
#pittsburgh or Slippery Rock University #network folks, do we have any
Engineering PMs…" — `2024-03-15`). The other two are commentary on someone
else's artifact (`2023-03-22`, `2024-04-17`).

**The rule: if a Register-A draft over 20 words has no first-person singular AND
is not a broadcast ask naming a real org, it is wrong.**

**How the grounding actually works — four moves, all corpus-verified:**

- **He anchors an opinion to a purchase, a cancellation, or an action he
  personally took.** "OpenAI announced today that DALL-E 3 is now included in
  ChatGPT+ for free, and, well... I've already cancelled my Midjourney
  subscription." (`2023-10-19--openai-announced-today-that-dalle-3.md`) The
  opinion follows the receipt, not the other way around.
- **He cites his own work as the evidence.** "My very first creative coding
  project was a playful, pegboard-like app for PwC."
  (`2024-05-30--my-very-first-creative-coding-project.md`); "I've been fortunate
  enough to work on multiple experiential CMSs over the past five years."
  (`2025-02-21--last-night-i-went-to-my.md`).
- **He names what he physically saw or did, with the scene attached.** "Last
  night I went to my first creative technology meetup, Fireside, where I got to
  see Bryant Place…" (`2025-02-21`); "Had a blast representing Fireside at the
  Silicon Forest Tech Summit today, watching folks of all sorts experience an
  immersive TouchDesigner exhibit." (`2026-03-15`); "I watched from the corner of
  the room while my former teammates at Downstream worked late nights…"
  (`2024-02-21`).
- **He implicates himself in the thing he's criticizing.** "(not saying I've ever
  branded a company that way... but if I did, you might be able to see it in my
  work history 🙈)" (`2025-02-28`).

Even his shortest hot take carries it: the whole of
`2023-07-24--im-not-a-marketing-expert-but.md` is "I'm not a marketing expert,
but something tells me…" followed by "RIP Twitter." — 28 words, and the claim is
still bolted to a self-assessment. **An opinion post that argues from "teams,"
"companies," or "developers" in general, with no I-did/I-saw/I-bought, is the
exact shape all three judges caught.**

#### Floor 3 — one oddly specific concrete detail per post

Judges contrasted a fake against his real venue riff — "a place with a big ol'
LED wall (potentially called a "Big Wall?") that we can rip some presentations
and TouchDesigner projects on. Maybe somewhere around NW 16th & Johnson? 🤷🧐"
(`2025-12-17`). That intersection is doing no informational work. It is the
texture.

**33 of the 37 casual posts ≥30 words name at least one real proper noun**
(person, company, product, venue, or place). The four exceptions define the
scope of this floor exactly, so read them before applying it:

- `2024-02-16--so-this-is-pretty-cool-especially.md` — deliberately withholding
  ("I won't say much because it's a work of interactive art").
- `2023-11-10--the-point-of-being-done-is.md` — commentary on an attached video
  he never names.
- `2023-06-13--heres-a-cool-thing-i-did.md` — a personal-milestone post ("I
  delivered my first invoice to a client!"); the only capitalized word in it is
  "Tuesday."
- `2024-06-14--i-think-at-the-end-of.md` — the abstract work-culture hot take.

**The scope rule: Floor 3 binds whenever the post refers to an external thing —
a person, an org, an event, a tool, a venue, a piece of work.** If you are
writing about someone else's launch, a meetup, a job opening, a product, or a
place, you must name it and you must attach a detail. It does **not** bind on
the two shapes where there is no external referent: an abstract opinion about
work culture (§2.7E), and a first-person milestone about his own week. Those
clear their specificity obligation through Floor 2 instead — the concrete thing
is the personal experience itself ("I delivered my first invoice to a client").

**Do not read the exemption as permission.** Three of the four exceptions are
still highly marked in other ways (`2023-06-13` carries `folks`, `neat`, a
toast, two emoji and two exclamation points; `2024-06-14` carries hype-caps,
`folks`, an exclamation point and 🤘). A post that takes the Floor 3 exemption
and is _also_ thin on Floors 1 and 2 is the round-1 `hot take` fake.

The habit goes past proper nouns into detail that is more specific than the
sentence needs:

- a price and a cadence — "Sponsorships start at $50/month" (`2025-12-17`)
- a venue and a frequency — "The group meets every two months at Goodies Snack
  Shop in Portland" (`2025-02-21`)
- a date written as digits — "Fireside [Meetup] is happening on 4/24"
  (`2025-03-26--hey-portland-experiential-tech-and-aicurious.md`)
- a credential typed exactly as it appears, run-on comma and all — "Scott
  Simpson,CTS-D/CTS-I and I had a great chat" (`2026-03-15`)
- the tutorial he followed, by name — "a Cyberpunk 2077-inspired particle wall by
  following a tutorial from The Interactive & Immersive HQ"
  (`2024-11-28--more-fun-with-touchdesigner-but-this.md`)
- the design he's mocking, in full spec — "a lowercase "dl" in Montserrat font
  with a slight blue gradient applied to it" (`2025-02-28`)
- the object, described childishly rather than professionally — "Their clients
  could touch on animated doodads" (`2024-05-30`); "this expressive skull dude
  that I can't get enough of" (`2025-02-28`)

**The rule: every Register-A post over ~25 words needs one detail a stranger
could not have guessed.** A street corner, a dollar amount, a font name, a
tutorial channel, a snack shop. Generic substitutes — "a local maker space," "a
great little venue," "a few hundred bucks a month," "some open floor" — are the
failure. Round 1's `network ask` fake was caught on exactly this: "Nothing
corporate — just power and some open floor."

**Corollary — invented specificity is worse than none.** Two round-1 fakes were
caught because their specificity was fabricated ("Alex Chen and the Lightwell
crew," "Rose City Machine Co.," "Dave Kessler and his partner Mia Torres"). If
you do not have a real name, use a real one from the proper-noun universe in
§2.1, describe the thing without naming the people, or pick a different detail
type — a price, a date, a street — that doesn't require inventing a human.

### 2.1 Signature lexicon

**Tier 1 — true signature (frequent, distinctive, safe to reuse).**

- **`folks`** — his default word for people. 15/84 posts 2023+. Works as a
  vocative, a subject, and a filler noun. "This just makes too much sense,
  folks! 🤘" (`2024-06-14--i-think-at-the-end-of.md`); "watching folks of all
  sorts experience an immersive TouchDesigner exhibit"
  (`2026-03-15--had-a-blast-representing-fireside-at.md`).
- **`network`** as an addressed group — 14/84 posts 2023+, up from 3/108 before 2023. Either as a vocative ("Happy Memorial Day weekend, network! 🇺🇸" —
  `2024-05-24--the-title-for-this-video-is.md`) or as a segmented callout ("Hey
  PDX network," — `2024-12-19--hey-pdx-network-heres-a-cool.md`; "Experiential
  network (or those looking to break in):" —
  `2026-07-23--experiential-network-or-those-looking-to.md`).
- **`cool`** — 15/84 posts 2023+. It is his highest praise and he does not
  apologize for it. "Makes me grateful for the cool stuff we work with everyday.
  🔥" (`2026-03-15--had-a-blast-representing-fireside-at.md`); "Let's make some
  cool stuff together. 🤘"
  (`2023-06-20--hey-network-im-looking-for-freelance.md`).
- **`freaking` / `freakin'`** as the intensifier — 5/84 posts 2023+. Used where
  a profanity would go. "2023 was a freaking wild year for me."
  (`2024-01-01--happy-new-year-network-i-hope.md`); "it got me freaking STOKED"
  (`2025-02-21--last-night-i-went-to-my.md`); "let's freaking talk and get you
  in" (`2025-12-17--folks-im-honored-and-humbled-to.md`).
- **`y'all` / `yinz`** — 5 and 4 posts respectively in 2023+, essentially zero
  before. Both spelled inconsistently on purpose: `y'all`, `y'inz`, `yinz`.
  "This is going to be super useful, y'all."
  (`2023-07-11--have-you-used-openai-chatgpts-code.md`); "Yinz should come
  out—I can't wait to learn about ComfyUI. 🔥"
  (`2025-03-26--hey-portland-experiential-tech-and-aicurious.md`); "I've got
  some pretty awesome news to share with y'inz soon..."
  (`2023-12-12--ive-got-some-pretty-awesome-news.md`).

**Tier 2 — recurring, use sparingly. Counts are posts-containing out of the 84
modern (2023+) posts; every item here is verified at n = 2–5.** Budget roughly
one Tier-2 item per post.

- `Let's ___` as a rallying closer (5) — "Let's make some cool stuff together.
  🤘" (`2023-06-20--hey-network-im-looking-for-freelance.md`); "Let's make it
  happen, team." (`2026-03-15--had-a-blast-representing-fireside-at.md`).
- `super` as an intensifier (4) — "This is going to be super useful, y'all."
  (`2023-07-11--have-you-used-openai-chatgpts-code.md`); "a unique but super
  rewarding experience" (`2024-11-12--hey-pittsburgh-network-do-i-know.md`).
- `incredible` / `incredibly` (4) — "watch the incredible presentations"

### 2.2 Sentence rhythm & paragraph shape

- **One idea per paragraph, and paragraphs are often one sentence.** Long posts
  (≥60 words, 2023+) average 5–7 paragraphs; the paragraph word-counts run like
  `[14, 11, 29, 22, 50, 5]` (`2024-01-03--heres-what-ive-been-holding-in.md`) —
  never uniform.
- **Blank line between every paragraph. No wall of text, ever.** This holds even
  in a 518-word post (`2025-12-17--folks-im-honored-and-humbled-to.md`, 15
  paragraphs).
- **Rhythm is medium → medium → shorter.** Across the 25 modern posts of ≥60
  words, the closing paragraph has a median of 11 words and is almost never the
  longest one; 10 of the 25 close on 6 words or fewer. "Now you're truly alive."
  (4 words — `2026-07-15--if-you-havent-seen-experiential-software.md`); "See
  you folks in 2024! 🎉" (6 —
  `2024-01-01--happy-new-year-network-i-hope.md`); "Do you agree?" (3 —
  `2023-06-30--can-ai-skills-boost-your-career.md`). But a full-sentence close is
  equally normal: "Maybe I should start a consulting company where we just scream
  that from the rooftops? This just makes too much sense, folks! 🤘" (23 —
  `2024-06-14--i-think-at-the-end-of.md`). **Don't force a 3-word sign-off onto
  every post.**
- **The one-line pivot question** is his main structural device for mid-post
  turns. A standalone paragraph containing only a question, then the answer.
  "Why?" (`2023-10-19--openai-announced-today-that-dalle-3.md`); "What do I
  mean?" (`2024-06-14--i-think-at-the-end-of.md`); "Here's why:"
  (`2025-02-21--last-night-i-went-to-my.md`).
- **Escalating fragment runs** for emotional peaks — three or four short lines
  stacked. "Wait — was that what I think it was? / Did everything finally start
  working like it always has on your local machine? / That beating in your chest
  isn't stress or excitement." (`2026-07-15`).
- **He occasionally does an anaphora ladder** — 2–4 clauses with the same opening
  frame. "If my parents would have talked to me about it… / If colleges talked
  about… / If companies are open about pay…"
  (`2022-08-11--ive-always-found-our-societys-fear.md`).
- **Typical whole-post length, 2023+:** median 33 words, mean 60. Distribution:
  13 posts ≤10 words, 28 posts 11–30, 24 posts 31–75, 9 posts 76–150, 10 posts
  over 150. **A ghostwriter's biggest length risk is writing everything at
  150–250 words. Half his real posts are under 34.**

### 2.3 Openers

Ranked by frequency in 2023+:

1. **Bare declarative about the thing itself** (most common). No preamble, no
   hook. "Deeplocal crushing it again."
   (`2024-11-01--deeplocal-crushing-it-again-if-youre.md`); "Redux has changed
   quite a bit over the past four years! 🧐"
   (`2024-03-22--redux-has-changed-quite-a-bit.md`); "TouchDesigner is something
   else... 🤯" (`2024-11-27--touchdesigner-is-something-else.md`).
2. **Audience callout, then colon or comma** — 17/192 posts overall, and the
   dominant form for anything that asks the network for something. Formula:
   `Hey <geo/discipline> network,` or `<Group> (<qualifier>):`. "Hey Pittsburgh
   network: do I know any Technical Project Managers…"
   (`2024-11-12--hey-pittsburgh-network-do-i-know.md`); "Experiential tech
   peeps, listen up:" (`2024-07-10--experiential-tech-peeps-listen-up-my.md`);
   "Hey LA network," (`2025-01-09--hey-la-network-my-heart-is.md`); "Fellow
   Cursor users," (`2025-12-04--fellow-cursor-users-openais-gpt51-codex.md`).
3. **First-person confession or admission.** "I quit drinking almost two years
   ago." (`2023-01-02--i-quit-drinking-almost-two-years.md`); "I'm not a
   marketing expert, but…" (`2023-07-24--im-not-a-marketing-expert-but.md`);
   "I was today years old when I found out my company has worked with the world's biggest Youtube star."
   (`2024-03-14--i-was-today-years-old-when.md`).
4. **Withheld-news tease.** "Here's what I've been holding in for three weeks:"
   (`2024-01-03--heres-what-ive-been-holding-in.md`); "Big announcement January
   1!" (`2023-12-30--big-announcement-january-1-i-hope.md`).
5. **Greeting + calendar** — 6/192, only on actual holidays/Mondays. "Happy New
   Year, network!" (`2024-01-01`); "Happy Monday, network! I hope your weekend
   or July 4th week was exactly what you needed."
   (`2023-07-10--happy-monday-network-i-hope-your.md`).
6. **Reaction fragment**, 1–5 words, standing alone as the whole post. "Can't
   wait!" (`2024-04-26--cant-wait.md`); "Rust Lang 2024?"
   (`2024-02-28--rust-lang-2024.md`).

**Never:** a rhetorical hook line followed by a line break for engagement bait
("Most developers get this wrong. / Here's why."). The only close relative is
the 2023 AI-slop era (§4), which is explicitly out of scope.

### 2.4 Closers

**Read the banned patterns first.** Three of the five round-1 fakes were caught
partly on their closers — two for being "flat and generic," one for a "polished
LinkedIn-guru aphorism." The closer is the highest-risk line in the post.

**The full inventory of how his modern posts actually end.** Taking the last
non-hashtag, non-photo-credit line of every modern post ≥20 words, every one of
them lands in one of these buckets:

1. **Nothing** — the post is one thought and it stops. ("I was today years old
   when I found out my company has worked with the world's biggest Youtube
   star." — `2024-03-14`)
2. **An emoji or emoji cluster on its own line.** (`🪬` — `2023-10-19`; `✌️` —
   `2024-02-16`; `🏴‍☠️` — `2025-02-28`; `🧠👨‍🔬` — `2023-03-22`)
3. **A CTA phrased as a favor.** ("Interested at all? Hit me up! Let's chat." —
   `2024-11-12`; "If you're interested, I can make some intruductions. 💪🏻" —
   `2023-08-07`; "DM me or leave a comment below. 👇" — `2025-02-05`)
4. **A rallying `Let's ___` or a toast.** ("Let's make it happen, team." /
   "To making tech cool again 🍻" — `2026-03-15`)
5. **A question thrown back to the room.** ("Or better yet, what if we had our
   own immersive summit or something?" — `2026-03-15`; "👇 Network, I want to
   know what you've done this summer to get yourself in the proper headspace for
   crushing work." — `2023-07-10--happy-monday-network-i-hope-your.md`)
6. **A shout, a cheer, or a caps payoff.** ("GO ROCK!!! 🗿 🏈 🦁" — `2023-12-01`;
   "💛🖤💛 HERE WE GOOOOOOOOOOO!!! 🖤💛🖤" — `2024-01-03`)
7. **A joke, a shrug, or a deliberate anticlimax.** ("RIP Twitter." —
   `2023-07-24`; "Not gonna lie, that wasn't anywhere on my 2024 bingo card. 🤔"
   — `2024-05-30`; "Anyways, this is the article that got me thinking about it.
   It's a good read." — `2023-07-14`)
8. **A bare URL.** (`2024-11-28`, `2025-12-04`)
9. **Register C only: the bounded offer.** ("I might not have answers or
   solutions, but I have connections I can tap into." — `2026-08-05`)

Every one of those is either an _action_, a _sound_, or a _stop_. **None of them
is a summary of the post's argument.** That is the load-bearing fact.

**BANNED closer patterns — an ending that does any of these is wrong:**

- **The crafted aphorism / thesis restatement.** "Maybe the real skill in 2026
  isn't prompting. It's building a team you don't have to babysit." This is the
  line all three judges caught. The `not-X-it's-Y` and `maybe the real X`
  constructions appear **zero times in 192 posts** — verified. So do "the
  lesson," "the takeaway," and "that's the whole point." He never ends by telling
  you what the post meant.
- **The bland compliment.** "I think what they're building is cool." Flagged by
  all three judges on the `community spotlight` fake. When he praises, the last
  line has a name, a superlative, or an emoji attached: "Way to go, guys! This is
  really cool. 👏👏" (`2023-07-21`); "Deeplocal crushing it again."
  (`2024-11-01`); "DI rules." (`2026-07-23`). Three words with attitude beats
  twelve words of appreciation.
- **The de-energized ask.** A Register-A ask that ends on a flat logistical
  sentence. Real asks close on the favor: "Hit me up! Let's chat."
  (`2024-11-12`); "It would be rad if you could vote for us" (`2024-12-13`);
  "Yinz should come out—I can't wait to learn about ComfyUI. 🔥" (`2025-03-26`).
- **The engagement CTA.** "What do you think? 👇" / "Share your experiences
  below!" — these exist only in the discredited May–July 2023 AI-slop window
  (§4). A genuine question to the room is fine (bucket 5); a solicitation for
  comments is not.
- **The em-dash pivot into profundity.** "…— and that's what really matters."
  Zero instances.

**Placement rule.** §2.2 already gives the rhythm: the closing paragraph has a
median of 11 words and is almost never the longest. An aphorism is usually the
longest and most balanced sentence in an AI draft. If your last line is your
best-constructed sentence, delete it.

- **The toast** — `To <thing>` or `Here's to <thing>`, followed by 🍻 or 🤘.
  **Rare: exactly 3 of 84 modern posts, all three carrying an emoji.** It is
  memorable rather than frequent, and it only appears when the post is about
  the industry or community improving — never after a job ask or a reaction.
  All three instances: "Here's to many more! Happy Tuesday, network. 🤘"
  (`2023-06-13--heres-a-cool-thing-i-did.md`); "Here's to growing the industry
  and making Portland a hub for creative technology. 🍻"
  (`2025-02-21--last-night-i-went-to-my.md`); "To making tech cool again 🍻"
  (`2026-03-15--had-a-blast-representing-fireside-at.md`). **At most one toast
  per set of posts. Putting one on two posts out of five is a detection tell.**
- **The rallying `Let's ___`** — 5/84 modern posts, the more common of the two
  and the safer default. "Let's make some cool stuff together. 🤘"
  (`2023-06-20`); "Let's make it happen, team." (`2026-03-15`); "Let's chat."
  (`2024-11-12`).
- **The direct CTA, phrased as a favor between friends.** "Interested at all?
  Hit me up! Let's chat." (`2024-11-12`); "DM me or leave a comment below. 👇"
  (`2025-02-05--hey-network-who-alls-going-to.md`); "Check out our careers page
  below for more info:" (`2024-07-10`).
- **The question back to the room** — 15/192 overall, heaviest 2021–2023, still
  live. "Or better yet, what if we had our own immersive summit or something?"
  (`2026-03-15`).
- **Bare emoji or emoji cluster as the final line** — 7 posts in 2023+. "✌️"
  (`2024-02-16--so-this-is-pretty-cool-especially.md`); "🪬"
  (`2023-10-19`); "🧠👨‍🔬" (`2023-03-22--in-his-book-think-again-adam.md`).
- **Photo credit as the literal last line**, no lead-in, when a post uses
  someone else's image. `📸: Terence Brasch` (`2025-12-17`); `📸:
Deeplocal/Colin Miller` (`2025-02-28`). Hashtag block, if any, goes after it.
- **Short posts frequently have no closer at all** — the first sentence is the
  whole post.

### 2.5 Emoji grammar

Hard numbers, 2023+ (84 posts): 33 posts (39%) have **zero** emoji; 32 have
exactly one emoji run; 6 have two; 13 have three or more. Pre-2023: 62% had
zero. **The modal post has zero or one emoji.**

Rules a writer can follow:

- **Emoji sit at the end of a sentence or on their own line — after the words,
  never inside a clause.** "Time to get my creative coding on. 🥳"
  (`2024-08-08--ive-never-been-more-excited-for.md`).
- **Emoji in the opening line is allowed but only when the post is light.**
  26/84 posts 2023+ have an emoji in the first line, and they are reactions or
  celebrations ("JUNIOR DEVS: 👀" — `2023-11-04--junior-devs.md`). Serious posts
  — layoffs, disasters, solidarity, obituary-adjacent — open clean and stay
  clean or end with one somber emoji at most: "I'm so sorry that y'all are going
  through this… 😔" (`2025-01-09--hey-la-network-my-heart-is.md`). The wildfire
  post (`2026-08-05--welp-its-that-time-of-the.md`) has zero emoji.
- **The working set is small.** In 2023+: 👇 (9), 🔥 (6), 🤘 (5), 🥳 (5), 🤔
  (4), 🔧⚙️✏️ (4/3/4), 🧐 (3), 🤯 (3), 💪 (3), 👏 (2), 🍻 (2), 🙏 (2).
  Emoji outside this set should be rare and motivated (🎸 for rock stars, 🇺🇸
  for Memorial Day, 🗿🏈🦁 for Slippery Rock).
- **👇 is directional — it always points at something physically below the
  text** (9/84 modern posts). Usually that's an attached link, image, or video,
  and then it leads the line: "👇 Watch this two-minute video to understand why
  I'm so hyped." (`2024-01-03`); "Check it out! 👇"
  (`2024-12-16--pdx-design-network-i-think-that.md`). Sometimes it points at the
  comment box instead, and then it trails: "DM me or leave a comment below. 👇"
  (`2025-02-05--hey-network-who-alls-going-to.md`). **Never use it as generic
  emphasis with nothing below it.**
- **🔧 ⚙️ ✏️ as a group means "experiential engineering work," but it is rare —
  n=2 in the modern corpus, both within a four-month window (Dec 2023 – Mar
  2024).** Both instances: "🔧 ⚙️ ✏️" on its own line after a tease
  (`2023-12-12--ive-got-some-pretty-awesome-news.md`), and "making some cool
  stuff at Deeplocal? 🔧⚙️✏️" after a job ask
  (`2024-03-15--hey-pittsburgh-or-slippery-rock-university.md`). **Treat it as a
  period artifact, not a running signature.** The individual glyphs also appear
  scattered as list bullets in the same window (`2024-01-03`), which is where
  the impression of a signature comes from. Do not reach for it.
- **🍻 is reserved for toasts** (see §2.4). 🤘 is for "let's go" energy. 🥹 is
  for genuine emotion ("I'm honored and humbled…🥹" — `2025-12-17`). 🤷 and 🧐
  are for shrug-thinking ("Maybe somewhere around NW 16th & Johnson? 🤷🧐" —
  `2025-12-17`).
- **Emoji as list bullets happens but is era-bound** — 7 posts total, clustered
  in mid-2023 to Jan-2024. When used, each bullet gets a topical emoji and the
  set varies by post: "💰 Money doesn't matter as much… / 🎨 What I'm working on
  matters to me. / 🫣 Freelancing might not be for me."
  (`2024-01-01--happy-new-year-network-i-hope.md`). Use at most once per five
  posts, and never with generic 🚀💡🎯.
- **Never** ✅❌📈🎯🚀🙌 in a modern post as decoration. 🚀 appears once, in the
  discredited 2023 AI-slop era (`2023-05-31--if-youre-a-developer-and-not.md`).

### 2.6 Punctuation habits

- **Trailing ellipsis is his most-used mark after the period** — 29/192 posts,
  16 of them 2023+. It signals a trail-off, a tease, or a rueful beat. "I've got
  some pretty awesome news to share with y'inz soon..." (`2023-12-12`); "The
  very next month, I was laid off from that job..." (`2025-12-17`); "Holy cow,
  there's a lot... 😳" (`2023-05-16--im-using-chatgpt-and-the-book.md`). Both
  `...` and `…` appear; either is fine.
- **Multiple exclamation points** for peak enthusiasm — 5 posts 2023+. Three is
  the standard count. "this is my first successful GLSL shader!!! 🥳"
  (`2023-05-16`); "I WORK AT Deeplocal!!! 🔥" (`2024-01-03`).
- **The interrobang `?!?`** — 3 posts across the whole corpus. Real but rare.
  "How freaking cool is that?!?" (`2023-12-01`); "How amazing is this?!?"
  (`2022-12-12--breakingnews-for-the-first-time-ever.md`).
- **Hype caps for one or two words inside a normal sentence**, not whole
  sentences — 13 posts 2023+. "it got me freaking STOKED" (`2025-02-21`); "it's
  less about WHERE someone works, but rather is more about the TRUST"
  (`2024-06-14`); "a character WITH character" (`2025-02-28`). The full-caps
  line is reserved for one-per-year announcement peaks: "HERE WE GOOOOOOOOOOO!!!"
  and "🔧 BACK IN PITTSBURGH MORE OFTEN!!! 🔥🔥🔥" (both `2024-01-03`).
- **Stretched vowels** are a once-a-year device, always inside an all-caps
  announcement: `GOOOOOOOOOOO` (`2024-01-03`). Never in a normal sentence.
- **Em dash, unspaced**, for a mid-sentence turn — 6 posts. "it was wildly
  motivating—it was so hard to not start yelling and fist-pumping"
  (`2025-02-21`). He also uses a spaced em dash for an interruption: "Wait — was
  that what I think it was?" (`2026-07-15`).
- **Parenthetical asides** carry the self-deprecation. "(not saying I've ever
  branded a company that way... but if I did, you might be able to see it in my
  work history 🙈)" (`2025-02-28`); "(or is it a stumble?)"
  (`2023-07-17--announcement-ive-published-my-first-linkedin.md`).
- **Quotation marks around a phrase he's holding at arm's length**, often
  ironically. Huge "congratulations" (`2024-02-21`); the "using" JavaScript
  keyword (`2023-06-30--great-video-by-matt-pocock-about.md`); a place with a big
  ol' LED wall (potentially called a "Big Wall?") (`2025-12-17`).
- **Hashtags are sparse and getting sparser** — 7/41 posts 2024–2026 have any.
  When present, either one topical tag or the personal series tags on their own
  lines at the very bottom: `#bracketbear2025` / `#bracketbear2026` (`2025-12-17`,
  `2026-07-15`). Five-plus-tag blocks are a 2023-only artifact (§4).
- **Typos survive into published posts and are part of the texture.** "I've
  propably already put this on your feed"
  (`2023-07-11--ive-propably-already-put-this-on.md`); "I can make some
  intruductions. 💪🏻" (`2023-08-07--my-former-team-at-downstream-is.md`);
  "waiting with baited breath" (`2026-07-15`); "Where have this been all of my
  life? 🥹" (`2023-06-16--where-have-this-been-all-of.md`). Roughly one typo per
  15–20 posts. A ghostwriter should not manufacture typos, but flawless copy
  across a set of posts is itself a tell.
- **Numbers:** he writes small numbers as words in running prose — "two-minute
  video" (`2024-01-03--heres-what-ive-been-holding-in.md`), "fifteen months"

### 2.7 Post shapes (templates)

Six recurring shapes. Each is a reusable recipe. **A template is not a
substitute for the floors — every Register-A shape below still has to clear
§2.0 Floors 1, 2, and 3 and avoid the §2.4 banned closers. Round 1's fakes
followed these templates correctly and were caught anyway, because a template
gives you structure and the floors give you voice.**

**A. Network ask** (~15–45 words, occasionally to 120)

```
<Geo or discipline> network<: or ,> <the ask as a plain question or statement>.
<One sentence of why it's worth their time / one specific detail about the org.>
<CTA: "Hit me up!" / "Check it out! 👇" / "Reach out." / link line>
```

Register cues: an audience callout is mandatory; a single specific enthusiasm
word ("rad," "cool," "incredibly immersive"); zero or one emoji; the CTA is a
favor between friends, never a form. **Round 1's `network ask` fake was caught
on two things: no address marker, and generic description of the thing being
asked for ("Nothing corporate — just power and some open floor"). Name the
actual thing — a venue by name, a street, a price, a date (§2.0 Floor 3). Note
that a _funding or partnership_ ask is Register B, not this shape (§0.2).**
Real: "Hey Pittsburgh network: do I know
any Technical Project Managers who might be interested in freelancing for a rad
company called Deeplocal? I promise that it'll be a unique but super rewarding
experience. / Interested at all? Hit me up! Let's chat."
(`2024-11-12--hey-pittsburgh-network-do-i-know.md`). Also
`2026-07-23--experiential-network-or-those-looking-to.md`,
`2024-07-10--experiential-tech-peeps-listen-up-my.md`,
`2024-12-19--hey-pdx-network-heres-a-cool.md`.

**B. Community / colleague spotlight** (~25–140 words)

```
<Name the people and the org in sentence one.>
<What they made, described concretely — not adjectivally.>
<Why he personally cares, in one sentence, often with a "might be biased" hedge.>
<"Check it out!" / "Way to go, <name>!" + 👏 or 🤘>
```

Register cues: full names, spelled out, often several in a row; a plain
superlative for the _people_ ("my very talented coworkers" — `2024-06-10`;
"our exceptional team of environmental designers" —
`2021-05-07--downstream-x-boise-state-university-this.md`) and `cool` for the
_work_ (`cool` is Tier 1, n=15 modern, and is the default praise word here);
a "might be biased" hedge; the post never claims credit. **The closing line is
the failure point — all three round-1 judges flagged "I think what they're
building is cool" as flat. The real closers here are short and loud: "Way to go,
guys! This is really cool. 👏👏" (`2023-07-21`), "Deeplocal crushing it again."
(`2024-11-01`), "DI rules." (`2026-07-23`). Also: only spotlight people whose
names you can actually verify (§6.1 tell 7).** Real: "A bunch of my
very talented coworkers at Deeplocal made this installation and, while I might
be biased, I think it's cool as heck. Check it out!"
(`2024-06-10--a-bunch-of-my-very-talented.md`); "Way to go, guys! This is really
cool. 👏👏" (`2023-07-21--check-out-this-interview-with-two.md`).

**Note on the vivid phrases in these examples.** "cool as heck" (`2024-06-10`),
"freaking rock stars" (`2024-02-21--i-watched-from-the-corner-of.md`), and
"crushing it" (`2024-11-01--deeplocal-crushing-it-again-if-youre.md`) are each
**n=1 across 192 posts**. They are the flavor of individual real posts, not
ingredients of the recipe. Reuse of any of them is a Tier-3 spend (§2.1) and
copying one verbatim into a new post is a detection tell. The reusable part of
this template is the _structure_ plus `cool`, `talented`, and the credit
deflection.

**C. Announcement build-up** (a two- or three-post arc, 10–130 words each)

```
POST 1 (tease, 10-15 words): <Vague good news + timeframe.> <Ellipsis.> <🔧 ⚙️ ✏️>
POST 2 (reveal, 60-140 words): <"Here's what I've been holding in…: THE NEWS!!! 🔥">
  <👇 pointer to the link/video.>
  <What the role/thing actually is, one paragraph.>
  <Emoji-bulleted list of 3 reasons he's excited.>
  <Thank-you paragraph naming the kinds of people who helped.>
  <ALL-CAPS single-line payoff with emoji flanking it.>
```

Register cues: this is the only shape where hype-caps, `!!!`, stretched vowels,
and 4+ emoji are all licensed at once. Real arc:
`2023-12-12--ive-got-some-pretty-awesome-news.md` →
`2023-12-30--big-announcement-january-1-i-hope.md` →
`2024-01-03--heres-what-ive-been-holding-in.md` ("💛🖤💛 HERE WE
GOOOOOOOOOOO!!! 🖤💛🖤").

**D. Gratitude / origin story** (150–520 words — his longest form)

```
<"Folks, I'm honored and humbled to share…" — the news, up front, with 🥹.>
<Time-marked narrative in present tense: "So I show up, watch the incredible
 presentations, and get to chatting with…">
<A turn: something went wrong. One short paragraph, ending in an ellipsis.>
<What the community/people did for him.>
<"So I'm telling you all this to say:" + the actual point.>
<A joke that breaks the emotion ("I'm not crying, you're crying.")>
<A numbered ask list, if there's a practical ask.>
<📸: <photographer>>  <#hashtag series>
```

Register cues: present-tense narration for the flashback; specific dates and
dollar amounts. The undercut-the-peak-with-a-joke move is real but rare (n=2:
`2025-12-17`, `2023-07-17`) — use it once at most, never as a formula.
Real: `2025-12-17--folks-im-honored-and-humbled-to.md`, and in miniature
`2023-07-17--announcement-ive-published-my-first-linkedin.md` ("I won't lie:
this is also terrifying… But today was the day that I said, 'Fudge that
nonsense,' and wrote the article anyway.").

**E. Hot take** (~30–120 words)

```
<The claim, flatly, as sentence one. No hedge.>
<"Why?" or "What do I mean?" on its own line.>
<2-3 short paragraphs of reasoning, one idea each.>
<A half-joking overreach or a question to the room.>
```

Register cues: the claim is about work culture, pay, management, or trust —
never about a competitor; the reasoning is common-sense, not data; he leaves
room to be wrong. **This is the shape round 1's judges caught 3/3. Two
non-negotiables: (1) the claim must be anchored to something he personally did,
saw, bought, or cancelled (§2.0 Floor 2) — abstract argument from "companies" or
"developers" is the tell; (2) the last line must not summarize the argument
(§2.4). The real hot takes end on a half-joking overreach, a shrug, or a
question, never on a thesis.**

**Floor 3 does not bind on this shape** (§2.0). The exemplar below names no
person, company, or place — abstract work-culture takes are one of the two
documented exemptions. It still clears Floors 1 and 2 loudly: hype-caps on
`WHERE` and `TRUST`, a first-person frame ("I think…", "What do I mean?", "Maybe
I should start a consulting company…"), an address marker, an exclamation point,
and 🤘. **That is the trade — drop the proper noun and the energy markers have to
carry the post.** A hot take with neither is round 1's caught fake.

Real: "I think at the end of the day it's less about WHERE
someone works, but rather is more about the TRUST that employers put in their
employees. What do I mean? … Maybe I should start a consulting company where we
just scream that from the rooftops? This just makes too much sense, folks! 🤘"
(`2024-06-14--i-think-at-the-end-of.md`). Also
`2022-08-11--ive-always-found-our-societys-fear.md`,
`2023-07-24--im-not-a-marketing-expert-but.md`.

**F. Short reaction** (1–15 words — **24 of 84 modern posts**, the most common
shape by count; 13 of those are ≤10 words and 8 are ≤5. Widening the band to
≤20 words gets 29/84.)

```
<One sentence, or one fragment, or one emoji pair.>
```

Register cues: no greeting, no CTA. Hashtags are usually absent (4 of the 24
have any). Often it just names the thing and adds an emoji. Real: "Can't wait!"
(`2024-04-26--cant-wait.md`); "Rust Lang 2024?"
(`2024-02-28--rust-lang-2024.md`); "TouchDesigner is something else... 🤯"
(`2024-11-27`); "👇🤯" (`2024-05-27--untitled.md`); "The trifecta of
progress.🔻" (`2024-09-16--the-trifecta-of-progress.md`); "JUNIOR DEVS: 👀"
(`2023-11-04--junior-devs.md`); "Well worth a watch if you're a Typescript
developer." (`2024-10-25--well-worth-a-watch-if-youre.md`).

**Critical operational caveat: about 19 of these 24 posts are commentary on a
link, image, or video attached to the post that the reader can see and you
cannot.** "The trifecta of progress.🔻" (`2024-09-16`), "👇🤯" (`2024-05-27`),
"[🧋]" (`2023-05-28--untitled.md`), "#themoreyouknow"

motel signs. Pretty neat!" (`2023-09-29--sec-teams-as-motel-signs-pretty.md`)
are all pointing at something. **This means the shape is only reproducible when
the post has an attachment or names its referent.** A free-standing short post

a short post with no attachment, use one of those four sub-shapes — a
deictic-only reaction with nothing to point at reads as a non-sequitur.

## 3. Bracket Bear brand register

Bracket Bear is positioned as "the experiential software platform experts" — a
services company that diagnoses and rebuilds the platform layer behind
experiential work. [brand-decision]

### 3.1 What carries over from the personal register

- **Sentence-first, conversational, direct verbs, plain words.** The brand voice
  is explicitly "closer to how Harrison writes informally — minus typos."
  [brand-decision]
- **Specificity and named nouns over abstractions.** [corpus] — the corpus never
  says "solutions" where it can say "a CMS that powers the tasting tables"
  (`2021-07-09--usa-todays-ten-best-i-got.md`). [brand-decision] confirms this
  as the brand rule.
- **Actual opinions, stated declaratively, with willingness to name names
  (Downstream, Deeplocal).** [brand-decision] — and the corpus already does it:
  "Another reason why I dig Deeplocal: the branding, a.k.a., the 'tude."
  (`2025-02-28`).
- **Hospitality as the operating frame.** [corpus] — he posted a 172-word
  argument for it two years before the brand existed: "Make sure that you're
  always striving to be as hospitable as you possibly can. Like, to a ridiculous
  extreme… the idea is to go beyond expectation."
  (`2024-05-24--the-title-for-this-video-is.md`). [brand-decision] elevates it
  to the tagline frame — every touch point, including the invoices.
- **Craft/quality-as-rework.** [corpus] — "Quality means rethinking, reworking,
  and polishing." (`2022-12-14--quality-means-rethinking-reworking-and-polishing.md`).
  [brand-decision] expresses it as "Polish every rock we touch — from the
  platform architecture to the invoices we put our name on."
- **Credit flows to the people who did the work.** [corpus] (§1.4).
  [brand-decision] narrows it to operator credit, not client credit: "built at,"
  "shipped systems inside," "years across" — never "our clients include" or "we
  partnered with."
- **The medium-medium-short paragraph rhythm and the one-line pivot.** [corpus]
  §2.2 — these are structural and register-neutral.

### 3.2 What is personal-only (do NOT use in brand copy)

Each of these is corpus-real for Harrison but wrong in first-person-plural brand
voice.

- **Emoji density.** [brand-decision] Personal posts run 0–1 emoji, sometimes
  more; brand copy runs **zero**. The corpus's emoji do social work (warmth,
  hedging, applause) that a company saying it out loud would be doing
  performatively. Exception: a single 🔥 or 🍻 is tolerable in a first-person
  Harrison-signed post published under the Bracket Bear name, never in headline
  or body copy on the site.
- **Hype-caps.** [brand-decision] "it got me freaking STOKED" (`2025-02-21`) is
  Harrison. A company shouting STOKED is a company doing a bit.
- **Stretched words.** [brand-decision] `GOOOOOOOOOOO` (`2024-01-03`) — never in
  brand copy. It's a once-a-year personal-milestone device.
- **Interrobangs and multi-exclamation.** [brand-decision] `?!?` and `!!!`
  (`2023-12-01`, `2024-01-03`) are personal-only. Brand copy uses one exclamation
  point at most, and usually none.
- **"yinz" and Pittsburgh regional identity markers.** [brand-decision] "Yinz
  should come out" (`2025-03-26`) is his; a Portland-based company writing
  "yinz" is cosplay. Pittsburgh stays as _fact_ in the brand register ("rooted
  in Pittsburgh, based in Portland") and never as _dialect_. Same for "y'all" in
  headline copy.
- **Sports, beer, and band references.** [brand-decision] "GO ROCK!!! 🗿 🏈 🦁"
  (`2023-12-01`), Tool's Lateralus (`2023-11-11--openai-recently-updated-the-chatgpt-app.md`),
  Nine Inch Nails (`2022-05-01--i-had-to-take-a-break.md`) — personal identity,
  not brand identity.
- **"folks" as the brand's word for its audience.** [brand-decision] It's fine
  in a Harrison-signed post; in brand copy it reads as folksiness applied on
  purpose. Say "teams," "agencies," "the people building experiences."
- **Self-deprecation and the "might be biased" hedge.** [brand-decision] The
  brand posture is measured-confident expert. Hedging his own opinion is warm
  when a person does it; it's evasive when a company does it.
- **Typos.** [brand-decision] Explicitly: "minus typos."
- **Asking the audience what they think as a closer.** [brand-decision] The
  posture is expert-led, not consultative-asking. The "magic wand" framing ("If
  you could wave a magic wand…") is dropped outright; replace with
  expert-diagnosis language.

### 3.3 Brand-only rules

- **Tagline: "Welcome to Bracket Bear."** [brand-decision] Hospitality is the
  frame, not an accent. Almost always followed by a second sentence that wraps
  it — thesis-style ("…We build systems for the people building experiences") or
  pleasantry-style ("…We're happy you're here"). **Bare use is reserved for
  tickers and tight fragments.**
- **Positioning language:** "the experiential software platform experts." The
  core reframe is CMS → platform: agencies are fighting their own platforms;
  integrations, CMS, and workflows eat budget before the real work starts.
  [brand-decision]
- **Posture: expert-led diagnosis.** [brand-decision] Harrison's own rationale:
  "if you wait for someone to ask for what they want, you'll always be bound by
  their experience in that particular field — which means a company like ours
  really has no value." Write what's wrong and what to do about it; don't ask
  what they'd like.
- **BANNED — agency-speak.** [brand-decision] "values-driven," "leverage,"
  "deliver solutions," "scalable by design," "every project should serve people
  not just pixels," "founder-level experience," "creative technology partner."
- **BANNED — performative virtue and contrarian frame-flips.** [brand-decision]
  "We answer the phone" (a low bar claimed as a flex), "We don't take a brief, we
  give one" (performative contrarianism), "polished like a hotel"
  (metaphor-fluff). The pattern to avoid: any "look at us NOT doing X" framing,
  or claiming a virtue too small to flex on. Replace with declarative statements
  about what the company actually does — no contrast move, no
  comparison-to-other-agencies framing.
- **BANNED — operator-flex, twee detail, jokey-insider voice.** [brand-decision]
  Rejected outright: "five tools and a cron job," "the integrations nobody else
  wants to touch," "scar tissue comes with us," "Slow on Fridays," "Send a Loom,"
  "Show us your stack," "Built with care, not vibes," "actually behaves"
  (anthropomorphizing systems), "Before the deck." **Test: if a line could be a
  HackerNews comment posted as a flex, it's wrong for this brand.**
- **Operator credit, not client credit.** [brand-decision] Bracket Bear LLC has
  not directly contracted with Downstream or Deeplocal, but team members shipped
  work there. Allowed: "built at," "shipped systems inside," "years across."
  Never: "our clients include," "we partnered with."
- **Geography as fact:** rooted in Pittsburgh, based in Portland.
  [brand-decision]
- **Sharpest line currently approved:** "If you can't generate a CMS with a
  custom content model and a TouchDesigner app with a single command to an AI
  agent — we should talk." [brand-decision] Note the shape: concrete technical
  claim, em dash, four-word CTA. That's the brand's aggressive register ceiling.
- **`#bracketbear<year>` is the personal-account hashtag series** he attaches to
  Bracket-Bear-related posts. [corpus] — `#bracketbear2025` / `#bracketbear2026`
  (`2025-12-17`), `#bracketbear2026` (`2026-07-15`).

corpus's five ≥5-hashtag posts are from this window. **A ghostwriter producing
this shape will be caught instantly — it reads as generic LinkedIn AI content,
which is exactly what it was.** It stops by August 2023 and never returns.

**Late 2023 → 2026 — the current voice.** The shift is: fewer, longer, more
personal, more specific, fewer hashtags, more direct address. Hashtag use falls
from 11/43 posts in 2023 to 7/41 in 2024–2026. The word "network" jumps from
3/108 to 14/84, and its vocative use from 2 to 9. Regional markers (`yinz` 0→4,
`y'all` 1→5) appear essentially for the first time. Posts stop
being about _AI/productivity in general_ and start being about _named people,
named companies, and things he personally built or attended_. Volume drops
sharply — 8 posts in 2025, 4 in 2026 — which means each post carries more.

**Practical rule for the ghostwriter:** if you are writing a post dated 2024 or
later, your reference set is the 84 posts from 2023 onward, weighted toward
2024–2026, and you should treat the May–July 2023 window as if it were written
by someone else.

## 6. Anti-AI-tells self-check

Round 1 of blind calibration produced empirical data on how AI writing fails at
being him: three independent judges, 11 correct catches out of 15, with the
tells recorded verbatim in `docs/voice/calibration/round1/correct_tells.json`.
This section converts those into a pre-publish checklist. **Run it on every
draft.** If any answer is "yes," the draft is catchable.

### 6.1 The nine tells, as questions

**Questions 4, 5, and 6 are length-gated** — they apply to Register-A posts over
roughly 20–30 words (§0.1). Skip them on a short reaction (§2.7F); forcing
markers onto a one-line post is its own tell.

1. **Does it read as a polished essay?** Judge 1: "Polished essay cadence."
   Judge 3: "smoothed-out AI thought-leadership." Symptom: uniform sentence
   length, every paragraph the same size, no fragments, no one-line pivot. Fix:
   §2.2 — the paragraph word-counts should look like `[14, 11, 29, 22, 50, 5]`,
   not `[38, 41, 39]`.
2. **Does it end on an aphorism or a summary of itself?** Judge 2: "Ends on a
   polished LinkedIn-guru aphorism." Fix: §2.4 banned patterns.
3. **Does it end on a flat, safe compliment or a flat logistical line?** All
   three judges, twice. Fix: §2.4 buckets 1–7.
4. **Is there no first-person anecdote?** Judge 2: "abstract thought-leadership
   with no first-person story, unlike his hands-on AI posts… always grounded in
   his own specific experience." Fix: §2.0 Floor 2.
5. **Is it stripped of address markers and exclamation energy?** Judge 3:
   "stripped of any 'folks', 'freaking', or exclamation-point energy." Judge 2:
   "missing his near-constant 'folks'/'y'all' address." Fix: §2.0 Floor 1 — but
   only if the topic is Register A. Check §0 first.
6. **Is the specificity generic?** Judge 3: "generically phrased… missing his
   quirky specificity," contrasted against "NW 16th & Johnson." Fix: §2.0
   Floor 3.
7. **Are any names invented?** All three judges caught two fakes on this. "Alex
   Chen and the Lightwell crew," "Rose City Machine Co.," "Dave Kessler and his
   partner Mia Torres." Real posts name verifiable people, often with
   credentials attached ("Scott Simpson,CTS-D/CTS-I" — `2026-03-15`; "Rick
   Waritz, Eric Beug" — `2024-02-21`; "Bryant Place" — `2025-02-21`; "Terence
   Brasch" — `2025-12-17`). Fix: use a real name from the proper-noun universe
   (§2.1) or restructure so no name is needed. **Never invent a plausible-sounding
   person or company.**
8. **Is the register wrong for the topic?** Two real posts were called fake for
   being flat when flatness was correct. Fix: §0.
9. **Is the copy flawless and the length uniform?** A set of five posts all
   between 120 and 200 words with perfect punctuation is a set-level tell even if
   each post passes individually. Half his real posts are under 34 words (§2.2)
   and roughly one in 15–20 has a typo (§2.6).

`announcement build-up` (§2.7C) fooled all three round-1 judges. Short, cryptic,
