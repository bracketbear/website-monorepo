# Bracket Bear — brand direction

Everything Bracket Bear has decided about how it looks and how it talks. If you
are writing copy for the site, designing a page, or building UI, this is the
document you need.

**Read this before writing brand copy.** For Harrison's _personal_ writing —
LinkedIn posts, anything signed by him as a person — use
[`../voice/VOICE.md`](../voice/VOICE.md) instead. They are different voices and
mixing them is the most common mistake (see [Personal-only](#personal-only-do-not-use-in-brand-copy)).

## Status: decided, not measured

`VOICE.md` is an empirical model — it was derived from 202 real LinkedIn posts
and tested in two rounds of blind judging. **This document is not that.** It is
a record of decisions Harrison made about a company voice that did not exist
yet, so there was nothing to measure and nothing to hold out. There is no
calibration test for this file and none is planned.

That is a deliberate choice, not a gap. A brand voice for a new company is a
design decision; the only meaningful test is whether the copy it produces gets
published or rejected. Treat the rules below as decisions to follow, not as
findings to verify.

Six of the voice rules _are_ corpus-grounded — they carried over from how
Harrison already writes. Those are marked `[corpus]`. Everything else is marked
`[decision]` and traces to brand work Harrison did on 2026-05-02 through
2026-05-04.

**Amended 2026-08-24.** The first version of this document was defined almost
entirely by subtraction: a long ban list and one approved sentence shape. The
homepage it produced ran twelve consecutive first-person-plural declaratives and
repeated one three-noun abstraction four times. Two new sections under
[Voice](#voice) — **Required: the additive rules** and **Permitted: clarified** —
fix that. Every ban below still stands except where those sections say otherwise.

## Positioning

Bracket Bear is **"the experiential software platform experts."** `[decision]`

A services company — not a project shop — that diagnoses and rebuilds the
platform layer behind experiential work.

**The core reframe is CMS → platform.** The thesis, which came out of
Harrison's own LinkedIn writing: agencies are fighting their own platforms.
Integrations, CMS, and workflows eat the budget before the real work starts.
Bracket Bear rebuilds that layer as one platform.

Team augmentation and consulting stay on the menu, but they are not the
headline.

**Posture: expert-led diagnosis, never consultative-asking.** `[decision]`
Harrison's reasoning, in his words:

> "If you wait for someone to ask for what they want, you'll always be bound by
> their experience in that particular field — which means a company like ours
> really has no value."

Write what is wrong and what to do about it. Do not ask the reader what they
would like. This is why the "magic wand" framing ("If you could wave a magic
wand…") was dropped outright from earlier drafts — it is consultative-asking,
which is the wrong posture for this positioning. Replace any magic-wand copy
with expert-diagnosis language.

**Geography is stated as fact:** rooted in Pittsburgh, based in Portland.
`[decision]`

**Operator credit, not client credit.** `[decision]` Bracket Bear LLC has not
directly contracted with Downstream or Deeplocal, but team members shipped work
there. This is a factual accuracy rule, not a style preference.

- Allowed: "built at," "shipped systems inside," "years across"
- Never: "our clients include," "we partnered with"

## Tagline

**"Welcome to Bracket Bear."** `[decision]`

Hospitality is the frame, not an accent — every touch point is part of the
experience: the invoices, the quality of the work, the software shipped.
Bracket Bear is itself an experience. Use the tagline as the page's bookend,
top and bottom.

**It is adaptable, and almost always takes a second sentence that wraps it:**

- Thesis-style — "…We build systems for the people building experiences."
- Pleasantry-style — "…We're happy you're here." / "…We're happy to have you."
- "So"-connector style.

**Bare use is reserved for tickers and other tight fragments.**

The operational expression of the hospitality frame is the line from Harrison's
LinkedIn copy: _"polish every rock we touch — from the platform architecture to
the invoices we put our name on."_

## Voice

### What carries over from how Harrison writes

- **Sentence-first, conversational, direct verbs, plain words.** The brand voice
  is explicitly "closer to how Harrison writes informally — minus typos."
  `[decision]`
- **Specificity and named nouns over abstractions.** `[corpus]` — his writing
  never says "solutions" where it can say "a CMS that powers the tasting tables"
  (`2021-07-09`).
- **Actual opinions, stated declaratively, with willingness to name names**
  (Downstream, Deeplocal). `[corpus]` — "Another reason why I dig Deeplocal: the
  branding, a.k.a., the 'tude." (`2025-02-28`).
- **Hospitality as the operating frame.** `[corpus]` — he argued for it at
  length two years before the brand existed: "Make sure that you're always
  striving to be as hospitable as you possibly can. Like, to a ridiculous
  extreme… the idea is to go beyond expectation." (`2024-05-24`).
- **Craft, understood as rework.** `[corpus]` — "Quality means rethinking,
  reworking, and polishing." (`2022-12-14`).
- **Credit flows to the people who did the work.** `[corpus]`, narrowed by
  `[decision]` to operator credit — see Positioning.
- **The medium-medium-short paragraph rhythm and the one-line pivot.**
  `[corpus]` — these are structural and work in any register. See `VOICE.md`
  §2.2.

### Personal-only — do NOT use in brand copy

Every item here is real for Harrison and wrong in a company's first-person
plural. The general principle: warmth that reads as genuine from a person reads
as performance from a company.

| Personal-only                                                                | Why it fails in brand copy                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Emoji.** Personal posts run 0–1; brand copy runs **zero**.                 | His emoji do social work — warmth, hedging, applause. A company doing that out loud is performing it. _Narrow exception:_ a single 🔥 or 🍻 in a first-person Harrison-signed post published under the Bracket Bear name. Never in headline or body copy on the site. |
| **Hype-caps.** "it got me freaking STOKED" (`2025-02-21`).                   | A company shouting STOKED is a company doing a bit.                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Stretched words.** `GOOOOOOOOOOO` (`2024-01-03`).                          | A once-a-year personal-milestone device. Never brand.                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Interrobangs and multi-exclamation.** `?!?`, `!!!`.                        | Brand copy uses one exclamation point at most, usually none.                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **"yinz" and Pittsburgh dialect.** "Yinz should come out" (`2025-03-26`).    | A Portland-based company writing "yinz" is cosplay. Pittsburgh stays as _fact_ ("rooted in Pittsburgh"), never as _dialect_. Same for "y'all" in headline copy.                                                                                                       |
| **Sports, beer, and band references.** Slippery Rock, Tool, Nine Inch Nails. | Personal identity, not brand identity.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **"folks" as the brand's word for its audience.**                            | Fine in a Harrison-signed post; in brand copy it reads as folksiness applied on purpose. Say "teams," "agencies," "the people building experiences."                                                                                                                  |
| **Self-deprecation and the "might be biased" hedge.**                        | Warm when a person does it; evasive when a company does it. The brand posture is measured-confident expert.                                                                                                                                                           |
| **Typos.**                                                                   | Explicitly: "minus typos."                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| **Asking the audience what they think as a closer.**                         | Expert-led, not consultative-asking. See Positioning.                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |

### Banned — agency-speak

`[decision]` Harrison flagged all of these in the existing CMS content as
patterns to remove:

> "values-driven," "leverage," "deliver solutions," "scalable by design," "every
> project should serve people not just pixels," "founder-level experience,"
> "creative technology partner."

### Banned — performative virtue and contrarian frame-flips

`[decision]` Harrison's word for these was "douchy." Rejected examples:

- **"We answer the phone"** — claims a low bar as a flex.
- **"We don't take a brief, we give one"** — performative contrarianism.
- **"polished like a hotel"** — metaphor-fluff.

**The pattern to avoid:** any "look at us NOT doing X" framing, or claiming a
virtue too small to flex on. The strike-through "We don't 'leverage synergies'"
device in an early manifesto draft falls under this rule and was dropped.

**Replace with:** declarative statements about what the company actually does.
No contrast move, no comparison-to-other-agencies framing. (Declarative is the
default shape, not the only permitted one — see **Permitted: clarified**.)

### Banned — operator-flex, twee detail, jokey-insider voice

`[decision]` Rejected outright during a copy audit:

> "five tools and a cron job," "the integrations nobody else wants to touch,"
> "scar tissue comes with us," "Slow on Fridays," "Send a Loom," "Show us your
> stack," "Built with care, not vibes," "actually behaves" (anthropomorphizing
> systems), "Before the deck."

These are not agency-speak — they are a separate failure mode: performing
toughness, insider-fluency, or contrarian cuteness instead of demonstrating
expertise. The pattern covers developer in-jokes, hardened-vet flexing, twee
humanizing details, brusque demand-style CTAs, anthropomorphizing systems, and
contrarian frame-flips.

**Why this is subtle:** specificity and direct verbs are still good — that is a
core voice rule. The failure is when specificity tips into showing off.

**How to apply:** default to sentence-shaped expert claims ("We rebuild that
layer as one platform") and the operational hospitality lines ("polish every
rock we touch") rather than punchy operator-detail one-liners.

**The test: if a line could be a HackerNews comment posted as a flex, it is
wrong for this brand.**

### Required: the additive rules

`[decision]` **Added 2026-08-24.** Bans alone produced flat, generic copy. These
are obligations rather than permissions — a draft that violates one is wrong even
if it breaks no ban.

1. **Every section carries at least one real named noun.** A tool, a runtime, a
   place, a company, a client's actual thing. "Content modeling, integrations,
   workflows" is not a named noun, it is a category list, and the v1 homepage
   shipped it four times. The standard is the corpus line already quoted above:
   "a CMS that powers the tasting tables."
2. **No two consecutive headlines open with "We."** The v1 homepage ran twelve
   first-person-plural declaratives from hero to bookend. That is what "use
   declarative statements" sounds like when it is the only approved shape.
3. **Specific enthusiasm is permitted, and it attaches to a named thing.**
   `[corpus]` — `VOICE.md` §1.1: enthusiasm is Harrison's baseline register and it
   never attaches to an abstraction. The brand may be visibly into TouchDesigner,
   a good content model, or someone else's work. It may not be excited about
   "solutions."

### Permitted: clarified

`[decision]` **Added 2026-08-24.** Three things this document banned by
implication rather than on purpose.

| Permitted                                                                                             | The line that still holds                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Second person.** Address the reader — "your producers," "what your stack is costing you."           | The **generic vocative** stays banned. "Leaders," "founders," "builders" appear zero times in 192 posts (`VOICE.md` §1.2). Name the reader by their situation, never by their job title.                                                                            |
| **Rhetorical questions.** A question that makes the reader recognize a situation they are already in. | **Consultative-asking stays banned**, and these are different moves. "If you could wave a magic wand…" asks the reader to supply the diagnosis. "Count the places a single content change has to be made before it's live" hands them one. The first is still dead. |
| **Specific enthusiasm** — see the additive rules above.                                               | Enthusiasm about an abstraction is still hype. Still zero emoji, zero hype-caps, one exclamation point at most and usually none.                                                                                                                                    |

**What did not change: the operator-flex ban holds.** "Five tools and a cron
job," "the integrations nobody else wants to touch," "scar tissue comes with us"
stay dead. Those were never what made the copy generic — the gap they left got
filled with nothing instead of with facts. A named noun beats a flex on
specificity, and it does not read as a HackerNews comment.

### The aggressive register ceiling

The sharpest line currently approved: `[decision]`

> "If you can't generate a CMS with a custom content model and a TouchDesigner
> app with a single command to an AI agent — we should talk."

Note the shape: concrete technical claim, em dash, four-word CTA. That is how
far the brand goes. (The functionality exists; there was no recorded demo as of
2026-05-03.)

We build systems for the people building experiences."                                                                            | "values-driven," "leverage," "deliver solutions," "scalable by design," "creative technology partner."                                                                   |

## Visual identity

### Aesthetic

**Orange-dominant.** `[decision]` Warm dark canvas with vivid orange as _the
page color_, not as an accent.

The reference is **Donkey Kong Country Returns silhouette levels** — bright
sunset behind, dark silhouettes in front. Gradient sun, warm rim lighting.

Tone is **"90s design concepts at modern fidelity"** — bold, graphic,
type-heavy. Explicitly **not** glitchy-everywhere.

### Palette

| Token     | Role                                                             |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `#FF5F1F` | Vivid orange — the page color                                    |
| `#110A08` | Warm dark ink                                                    |
| `#FFF3E3` | Cream, for paper highlights                                      |
| `#7DF9FF` | Cyan — reserved as "tape damage" only, roughly 5% of the surface |

The brand notes also list `#0E0D10` as an ink color, but it does not appear in
the canonical draft (verified 2026-08-22) — `#110A08` is the one actually in
use.

### Type

| Face              | Role                                                             |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Anton**         | Display, all caps                                                |
| **Archivo Black** | The BRACKET BEAR wordmark — deliberately distinct from headlines |
| **Space Grotesk** | Body                                                             |

The historical site used Fira Sans + Barlow Condensed. Both are replaced.

### Design system source of truth

**`apps/bracketbear-website/public/draft/index.html`** is the canonical
reference, especially the **header (nav + status ticker)**. `[decision]`

Harrison has tweaked the styling there directly and treats it as locked. When
working on Bracket Bear UI: **do not propose alternative visual treatments.**
Describe content placement that fits the existing draft's classes —
`.hero__eyebrow`, `.hero__punch`, `.offer`, `.value`, `.manifesto__lines`,
`.cta__grid`.

New elements — the Receipts section, the platform-layers sub-strip, the welcome
bookend at the closing CTA — are visual judgments left to Harrison.

_Last verified 2026-08-22: the draft exists, is unchanged since 2026-05-03, and
carries every token and typeface listed above._

### Reusing the logo animation

The "glitchy logo" treatment (logo-particle-sphere with cyan/green/purple)
gives the right feel, but those colors fight the brand. Recolor to the palette
above — orange + cream + ~5% cyan damage — when reusing it.

## Why all of this

The existing site read as generic agency boilerplate. The brand should be
confident, opinionated, and built on craft. Harrison is the founder; the voice
should sound like him — which is why so many of the rules above are phrased as
"his thing, minus the parts that only work coming from a person."
